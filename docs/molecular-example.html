Assay and label definition

Teaching example with synthetic labels. A reference is labelled active when its structure has a hydroxyl bonded directly to an aromatic carbon. The inactive references have no phenolic hydroxyl. The labels encode this structural rule and do not represent measured biological activity.

Why this label?

The test is 4-fluorophenol and lies on the active side because its OH is directly bonded to the aromatic ring.

What distinguishes the labeled references
Active references have an OH group bonded directly to an aromatic carbon, while inactive references lack that phenolic OH.
Chemical rationale
The closest same-scaffold reference, active p-cresol, has one fewer halogen and preserves the phenolic OH, and the para-H analog phenol is also active. Methylating phenol gives inactive anisole, so loss of the phenolic hydrogen-bond donor explains that local label change. Benzyl alcohol is an inactive donor, confirming that donor status alone is insufficient and that direct aromatic attachment determines the synthetic label.
