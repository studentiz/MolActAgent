We are studying three kinds of iris flowers: Iris setosa, Iris versicolor,
and Iris virginica. Botanists tell them apart mostly by looking at the
petals.

  - Setosa flowers have very short, narrow petals and are easy to spot;
    they look quite different from the other two species.
  - Versicolor flowers have medium-long, medium-wide petals.
  - Virginica flowers have the longest and widest petals; they overlap
    a fair amount with versicolor, especially on the smaller end of
    virginica and the larger end of versicolor, so deciding between
    those two is the harder boundary in this dataset.

The sepals (the green leaf-like parts under the flower) overlap more
across all three species, so they are weaker clues than the petals.
Our goal is to look at a flower's measurements and decide which of the
three species it most likely belongs to, and to explain *why* using the
petal/sepal numbers — including being honest when a flower sits near
the versicolor / virginica boundary.
